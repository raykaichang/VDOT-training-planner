# Hansons training-plan research

Research date: 2026-08-03 (Asia/Taipei)

This research uses first-party material downloaded from Hansons Running Shop and Luke Humphrey Running. Luke Humphrey is the author/co-author of the Hansons method books and the training-plan PDFs published by Hansons Running Shop link back to his coaching resources. The PDFs remain local research files and are excluded from the repository; this document preserves source links, notes, and checksums without redistributing the files.

## Direct answer

Yes, Hansons-based plans exist for distances at or below the half marathon:

- Half marathon: free Classic Beginner and Classic Advanced 18-week PDFs are published by Hansons Running Shop.
- 10K: Hansons Running Shop publishes a free 20-week "Couch Potato to 10K" completion plan. Luke Humphrey Running separately sells Hansons-inspired Just Finish, Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, and Elite 10K plans.
- 5K: Luke Humphrey Running sells Hansons-inspired plans from true beginner through elite. There is no free Classic 5K PDF on the Hansons Running Shop download page.

The 5K and performance 10K plans must not be generated by scaling the marathon schedule. First-party coaching guidance explicitly changes the recovery model, workout priority, periodization, and long-run role by race distance.

## Source hierarchy

1. **Hansons Running Shop** - the Hansons organization's free Classic plan downloads. This is the strongest source for the exact free schedules.
2. **Luke Humphrey Running (LHR)** - primary methodology source from the Hansons method author/coach. It is the strongest public source for 5K/10K logic and the current paid-plan catalog. The current catalog describes these as "Hansons-inspired," so this report does not call every LHR product a free Classic Hansons plan.
3. Secondary blogs, forum posts, and unofficial schedule transcriptions were excluded from the implementation conclusions.

## Core logic shared by the method

The marathon and half-marathon methods are built around cumulative fatigue: consistent training creates a controlled buildup of residual fatigue without crossing into overtraining. The five interacting components are weekly mileage, intensity, balance, consistency, and recovery/active recovery.

Training days fall into two broad classes:

- Easy running, used both for aerobic volume and active recovery.
- "Something of Substance" (SOS), including speed, strength, race-pace tempo, and long runs.

The practical rules are:

- Distribute mileage across the week instead of concentrating most volume in one long run.
- Do not place hard workouts back to back; use easy running between them.
- Prescribed pace is a physiological target, not a challenge to run as fast as possible.
- Treat the plan as an interacting system. Adding distance or intensity to one workout changes recovery and the quality of later workouts.
- Select a plan primarily by sustainable peak weekly volume and available weeks, not only by a Beginner/Advanced label.

## Distance-specific engines

### Marathon

The two free Classic plans are 18 weeks.

- Primary weekly pattern after the introductory weeks: Tuesday speed/strength, Thursday goal-marathon-pace tempo, Sunday long run, easy running between SOS days, and Wednesday rest/cross-training.
- Speed block: 400 m through 1,200 m repeats at 5K-10K pace.
- Strength block: longer repeats such as 6 x 1 mile, 4 x 1.5 miles, 3 x 2 miles, or 2 x 3 miles, generally at goal marathon pace minus 10 seconds per mile.
- Tempo progression: goal marathon pace, building from 5 to 10 miles in Beginner and 6 to 10 miles in Advanced.
- Long run: commonly alternates shorter weeks with 14-16 mile weeks; both free plans top out at 16 miles.
- Peak published weekly volume: approximately 57.5 miles for Beginner and 61.5 miles for Advanced.
- Main difference between free levels: Advanced introduces SOS work earlier and starts from higher weekly mileage; Beginner uses a longer easy-volume introduction.

The 16-mile ceiling is not a standalone rule. It works only in the context of total weekly volume, preceding fatigue, and the following workouts.

### Half marathon

The two free Classic plans are 18 weeks and keep the same general Tuesday/Thursday/Sunday SOS rhythm, but use half-marathon-specific targets.

- Tuesday speed phase: 400 m through 1,200 m repeats around 5K-10K pace.
- Tuesday later phase: Beginner uses longer repeats at goal half-marathon pace minus 10 seconds per mile; Advanced prescribes the later long repeats at 10K pace.
- Thursday tempo: goal half-marathon pace, progressing from 3 to 6 miles in Beginner and 3 to 7 miles in Advanced.
- Long run: Beginner reaches 12 miles; Advanced reaches 14 miles.
- Peak published weekly volume: approximately 47 miles for Beginner and 50 miles for Advanced.
- Beginner delays its first speed session until week 6; Advanced starts speed and HMP work in week 2.

The PDFs display race-week total mileage as if race day contributes 15 miles, while the visible race-day cell only says "Race Day!" Treat this as an ambiguous session-total convention (possibly race plus warm-up/cool-down), not as a 15-mile race. The product must explicitly store a 21.0975 km race and separately model any warm-up/cool-down.

### 10K performance plan

LHR's first-party 10K philosophy says the event is roughly 90% aerobic for many runners, with the aerobic share increasing for slower finishers. It recommends a general-to-specific progression:

- Base: sustainable moderate-to-high weekly volume and periodic long runs.
- Introduction: light 5K-pace work plus a second session starting around half-marathon pace (or marathon pace in a longer block).
- Main block: one 5K-pace workout, one lactate-threshold/critical-velocity workout, and long runs at regular intervals. The main block should generally not exceed about eight weeks.
- LT/CV work: a 20-25 minute threshold tempo near one-hour race pace, plus longer repeats commonly around 8K-10K pace.
- Aerobic-power work: 2-5 minute bouts around mile-to-3K pace with roughly equal recovery.
- Faster sharpening is swapped in for another workout rather than added as a third hard workout.
- Cumulative fatigue is deliberately reduced compared with marathon/HM training; recovery from high-intensity work has greater priority.

The public paid catalog has Just Finish (14 weeks), Beginner (12), Intermediate (12), Advanced (13), and Elite (13) plans. Exact paid calendars were not copied.

### 10K completion plan

The free "Couch Potato to 10K" plan is a separate 20-week walk/jog progression, not the performance engine above.

- Begins with three 30-minute walking days.
- Progresses to four and then five activity days per week.
- Gradually lengthens continuous jogging from 3-minute segments to a 45-minute Sunday jog.
- Race week reduces the Sunday jog to 30 minutes, includes an off day two days before the race, and a 10-minute jog bracketed by 5-minute walks the day before.

The PDF does not prescribe 10K race-pace, threshold, or VO2max sessions. The UI should label it clearly as a beginner completion plan.

### 5K performance plan

LHR's first-party 5K philosophy recommends:

- A dedicated block of roughly 10-12 weeks for year-round runners: about 6-8 weeks of building work at pace, followed by about four weeks of sharpening.
- Moderate mileage, often around 80-90% of the runner's peak marathon volume, with very easy running between quality sessions.
- A progression from lactate-threshold repeats and 10K pace toward 5K, 3K, and occasional mile-pace work.
- Full recovery between the harder, higher-intensity workouts; cumulative fatigue is not the primary mechanism here.
- Long runs earlier in the block, then reduced/spaced out as race-specific intensity becomes the priority.

The public paid catalog lists True Beginner (20 weeks), Just Finish (14), Beginner (12), Intermediate (12), Advanced (13), and Elite (13). Exact paid calendars were not copied.

## Recommended product model

Add `planMethod = daniels | hansons`, then select a separate Hansons generator by `targetDistance`:

| Target | Generator | Required inputs | Primary progression |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5K | `hansons5k` | goal time/pace, current and peak volume, available weeks, level | LT/10K -> 5K -> 3K/mile sharpening; full recovery between hard days |
| 10K completion | `hansons10kCompletion` | current continuous-run ability, days/week, race date | walk/jog duration progression |
| 10K performance | `hansons10k` | goal time/pace, current and peak volume, available weeks, level | base -> 5K + LT/CV -> faster sharpening; 1-2 SOS days |
| Half marathon | `hansonsHalf` | goal HMP, current/peak volume, weeks, level | 5K-10K speed -> longer HMP-10/10K repeats + HMP tempo + 10-14 mi long run |
| Marathon | `hansonsMarathon` | goal MP, current/peak volume, weeks, level | 5K-10K speed -> MP-10 strength + MP tempo + cumulative-fatigue long run |

Additional implementation guardrails:

- Keep goal-race pace separate from the current Daniels VDOT training zones. VDOT may help estimate an initial goal, but the Hansons schedule keys important work to explicit goal MP/HMP and race-distance paces.
- Store warm-up, work, recovery, cooldown, and race distance as separate segments; do not infer them from a displayed weekly total.
- Let users choose sustainable peak volume and weeks. Level labels can remain as presets, not the primary algorithm.
- Preserve at least one easy/recovery day between hard workouts. Do not auto-stack missed SOS sessions.
- Treat 5K, 10K completion, 10K performance, half marathon, and marathon as different templates with shared primitives, not one scaled template.
- Do not reproduce paid LHR plans verbatim. Implement the public training principles and the free schedules, cite the method, and keep the downloaded book samples as internal research references unless redistribution rights are obtained.
